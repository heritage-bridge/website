Our Story

Preservation
without nostalgia

How a small group of students, engineers and historians began building a digital record of places that were disappearing faster than they could be studied.

I — The beginning

A church with a clock,
and no reliable drawings.

In 2022, when we first walked into Al-Saa'a Church in Mosul's old city, the clock tower that gave the church its name had been silent for years. Walls were scarred, the roof partially collapsed. What struck us most was not the damage — it was that no accurate measured record of the building existed anywhere.

We had one borrowed DSLR, a laptop that could barely run photogrammetry software, and permission for three afternoons. The resulting model was rough, but it could be rotated, measured and shared. For the first time, people who could not return to the church could see it again.

II — Why digital heritage

Memory is fragile.
Measurement is not.

Heritage disappears in two ways: suddenly, through conflict and disaster, and slowly, through neglect and the loss of the people who remember it. Digital documentation cannot stop either. What it can do is create an exact, shareable record — a geometry, a texture, a set of coordinates — that survives independently of the building.

We work in 3D because a photograph shows a view; a model holds a place. It can be measured, compared over time, used for reconstruction, taught from, and turned into experiences that reach people who will never visit.

"Documentation is not the end of care for a place. It is where informed care begins."

— Founding note, 2022

IV — Future vision

An open record
that outlives us

Over the next years we aim to publish one hundred documented sites across the region, establish the Academy as a recognised training route, and hand the archive to institutions that will maintain it long after the founding team has moved on. The bridge is meant to be crossed by others.

V — Identity system

Three marks, one modular identity

The name Heritage Bridge is a working title. Each concept is built so that the wordmark can be replaced while the symbol, palette and typography remain.

The Survey Arch
